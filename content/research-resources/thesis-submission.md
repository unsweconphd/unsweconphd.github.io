Submitting your thesis starts the examination rather than ending it. The sequence is: prepare the thesis, lodge your intention to submit, submit through GRIS, your examiners report, you defend the thesis at an [oral examination](/research-resources/oral-examination/), you make the corrections the examiners ask for, and the final thesis is deposited in the Library. The degree is awarded at the end of that, not on the day you submit.

A PhD must be completed within 4 years of full-time study. If you started in Term 1, plan to submit around September of Year 4.

## Two months before you submit

Lodge a **Notification of Intention to Submit** in the Graduate Research Information System ([GRIS](https://gris.unsw.edu.au/login)) at least two months before you plan to submit, so that the University has time to appoint your examiners.

The faculty then appoints your examination panel: two examiners from outside UNSW, and a panel chair from UNSW who runs the examination but does not examine you. For a doctoral thesis, at least one examiner is normally from outside Australia. You will be told their names, and from that point until the examination is over, neither you nor your supervisors may contact the examiners.

Your primary supervisor must also complete a Supervisor's Certificate in GRIS before the thesis can go to the examiners. Among other things they confirm that the final version has been checked with iThenticate, and that any publications are included in line with UNSW's rules.

## Formatting the thesis

The [Thesis Format Guide](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/research/higher-degree-research/thesis-format-guide.pdf) governs the format. The requirements people most often miss:

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | Up to 100,000 words, counting chapters and footnotes, not counting the preamble, tables, bibliography or appendices |
| Type | Body text 11 pt or larger, footnotes 10 pt, line spacing 1.15 or 1.5 |
| Publications list | List the publications and presentations that came out of your research during your enrolment, in the front matter, before the body of the thesis. This is in addition to what you declare in GRIS |
| Acknowledgements | Disclose all editorial assistance, including changes a publisher's editors made to a chapter you have published, and any use of AI, in enough detail for examiners to see what you used, how, and with what oversight. Keep personal acknowledgements to a page |
| RTP recipients | If you hold an RTP stipend or fees offset, the prescribed RTP sentence is mandatory. Copy it from the current Format Guide rather than from an older thesis: it now includes a DOI |
| Keep out of the thesis | No signatures, and do not reproduce the abstract, the Originality Statement or the Inclusion of Publications Statement. Those are completed in GRIS |

If any of your chapters are papers, read [using publications in your thesis](/research-resources/using-publications/) before you settle the structure. Your supervisor and the PGC have to approve any paper included in place of a chapter.

## Code, data and anything else to be examined

Everything the examiners are meant to assess must be hosted on a UNSW-managed platform. **A link to a GitHub repository is not acceptable**, and nor is any other external site: the examiners have to be able to read exactly what you submitted, unchanged.

Upload a fixed, documented snapshot of the exact version used in the thesis as additional files in GRIS — usually a self-contained `.zip` — and signpost it clearly in the PDF so the examiners know it is there and what to do with it. Each file may be up to 500 MB. Avoid formats that need commercial software. If the material cannot be hosted in GRIS at all, get the Graduate Research School's approval first.

## Permissions and public access

Get written permission to reproduce any third-party material **before** you submit for examination. Arrange it early; copyright questions go to <copyright@unsw.edu.au>.

Your final thesis is deposited in the UNSW Library and made publicly available, and depositing it is a condition of the degree being conferred. **No redactions to the final thesis are permitted.** If your research involves confidential, commercially sensitive or culturally sensitive material, plan the structure of the thesis for public access with your supervisor before you submit, rather than expecting to cut material out afterwards.

You may request an embargo of up to two years when you deposit the thesis. An embargo longer than two years needs the Dean of Graduate Research's approval and must be requested before you submit.

## Submitting

Submit one PDF of the whole thesis through GRIS, together with:

- the thesis title and the abbreviated abstract that will appear on your Australian Higher Education Graduation Statement;
- an **Originality Statement**, declaring the work is yours and that contributions to it are acknowledged in the thesis;
- an **Inclusion of Publications Statement**, saying whether the thesis contains publications and what you contributed to them. Everyone completes this, including candidates with no publications at all.

What happens after that is on [oral examination and outcomes](/research-resources/oral-examination/).

For the full rules, see the UNSW [HDR Examination Instruction](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/governance/policy/hub/hdrexamination.pdf), the [Thesis Format Guide](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/research/higher-degree-research/thesis-format-guide.pdf) and the [Higher Degree Research Policy](https://www.unsw.edu.au/governance/policy/policy-hub/hdrpolicy).
