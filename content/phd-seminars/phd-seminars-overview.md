## Format of PhD Seminars

Each student in years 1 - 3 must deliver **two presentations** over the academic year. Each student in year 1 - 3 will also be a **discussant** for **one** student presenter.

- **Presentation 1**
  - **Two weeks before your seminar, you must send your title and abstract to Sussan Su (<q.su@unsw.edu.au> AND <econ.hdr@unsw.edu.au>) so that invitations can be sent out to the faculty.**
  - **One week before** your presentation you must:
    - Send out the material (draft of paper and/or slides) to your discussant.
  - **Two days before** your presentation you must:
    - Circulate the title, draft paper, slides of your talk to **all Phd Students** and the PGRC.
    - If you store your material inside the shared One-Drive folder, you can just share it via email.
    - If you use Outlook, the mailing list tag is **#BUS School of Economics Research Students.**
    - You are encouraged to approach the discussant of your paper to exchange ideas. Engaging with your discussant prior to your seminar will help you make the most of their feedback (and can also help you deliver a better presentation).
  - **At the day of your presentation**:
    - You will have **40 minutes** to present your work.
    - Leave **10-15 min** for discussion session led by the discussant.
    - If you are the discussant, remember that your role is **not** just to summarise the presentation, but rather to offer meaningful feedback.
    - You may also expect clarification questions from the audience as go along with your presentation.
    - For job market presentations, you will have **90 minutes** to present your work. Be prepared for lots of questions and discussion during your talk, so aim for a presentation that lasts 70 or 75 minutes.
    - There is no discussant for job market presentations, however, you should expect questions from faculties and PhD students attending your seminar.
- **Presentation 2**
  - The idea of presentation 2 is the revise your work based on the feedback and comments you receive from the previous presentation. Usually there is a gap of at least 10 weeks between the two presentations.
  - The discussant should keep track of the comments given in the first presentation and also comment on whether they are addressed in the second presentation.

Each student in year 4 is required to **present their job market paper twice**, once in Term 1, and again in Term 2. The aim is to prepare for job talks.

## Rules and Responsibilities

### General rules

- Presentation and attendance are both parts of **APR milestone**: minimum 80% attendance is mandatory.
  - Students with less than 80% attendance must provide justification to the PGRC.
- Everyone in years 1 - 3 is required to deliver two presentations a year.
- Everyone in years 1 - 3 is required to serve as a discussant at least once a year.
- Everyone in year 4 is required to deliver two seminars.
- Dates are allocated by PGRC in coordination with the PhD student representative.
  - Please see the [2026 schedule](/phd-seminars/seminar-schedule/).
- You can swap slots when mutually agreed and approved by PGRC.

### Responsibility of Presenter

- Invite at least one academic staff.
  - First year PhD students: invite one of your supervisors.
  - Second year PhD students and above: invite at least one academic who is NOT your supervisor.
  - You may consider inviting people from other universities.
- You are required to present your own work.
  - If you are in your first year, you may present preliminary results or replication results.
- You may not present the same project that you have previously presented at the PhD seminar unless you have significant new developments (or if it is your JMP for 4th years).

### Responsibility of Discussant

- Prepare a set of slides that include:
  - summary of the talk (1 page max);
  - constructive criticisms, questions about the motivation, (interpretation of the) results, etc.

### Responsibility of Other Participants

- Please actively engage in the presentation. Try to be as critical (note: not mean) as possible - bonus points if you can pick apart the substance of the paper (i.e. its motivation, contribution). The aim is to help the presenter improve their research as much as possible.
- If you're attending via zoom, you are expected to **turn on your camera**.
