## Time and Place

- Times and rooms for each term are on the [seminar schedule](/phd-seminars/seminar-schedule/).
- **Zoom link**: to be provided by the presenter.

## Who Takes Part

- Each student in Years 1–3 must deliver **two presentations** over the academic year, and will also be a **discussant** for **one** student presenter, at both of that presenter's talks.
- Each student in Year 4 is required to **present their job market paper twice**, once in Term 1 and again in Term 2. The aim is to prepare for job talks.
- Master of Pre-Doctoral Business Studies (MPDBS) students attend the seminars, but do not present or act as discussants.
- Everyone, including MPDBS students, must attend at least **80%** of the seminars.

## Format of PhD Seminars

- **Presentation 1**
  - **Two weeks before your seminar, you must send your title and abstract to Sussan Su (<q.su@unsw.edu.au>) and to <econ.hdr@unsw.edu.au>, so that invitations can be sent out to the faculty.**
  - **One week before** your presentation you must:
    - Send out the material (draft of paper and/or slides) to your discussant.
  - **Two days before** your presentation you must:
    - Circulate the title, draft paper and slides of your talk to **all PhD students** and the Postgraduate Research Coordinator (PGRC).
    - If you store your material inside the shared OneDrive folder, you can just share it via email.
    - If you use Outlook, the mailing list tag is **#BUS School of Economics Research Students.**
    - You are encouraged to approach the discussant of your paper to exchange ideas. Engaging with your discussant prior to your seminar will help you make the most of their feedback (and can also help you deliver a better presentation).
  - **On the day of your presentation**:
    - You will have **40 minutes** to present your work.
    - Leave **10–15 minutes** for a discussion session led by the discussant.
    - If you are the discussant, remember that your role is **not** just to summarise the presentation, but rather to offer meaningful feedback.
    - You may also expect clarification questions from the audience as you go along with your presentation.
    - For job market presentations, you will have **90 minutes** to present your work. Be prepared for lots of questions and discussion during your talk, so aim for a presentation that lasts 70 or 75 minutes.
    - There is no discussant for job market presentations; however, you should expect questions from faculty and PhD students attending your seminar.
- **Presentation 2**
  - The idea of presentation 2 is to revise your work based on the feedback and comments you received at your first presentation. Usually there is a gap of at least 10 weeks between the two presentations.
  - The discussant should keep track of the comments given at the first presentation and also comment on whether they are addressed in the second presentation.

## Rules and Responsibilities

### General rules

- Presentations, discussant duty and attendance are all part of your **confirmation and APR milestones**: minimum 80% attendance is mandatory.
  - Students with less than 80% attendance must provide justification to the PGRC.
- Everyone in Years 1–3 is required to deliver two presentations a year.
- Everyone in Years 1–3 is required to serve as a discussant for one presenter each year, at both of that presenter's talks.
- Everyone in Year 4 is required to deliver two seminars.
- MPDBS students must meet the attendance requirement, but do not present or serve as discussants.
- Dates are allocated by the PGRC in coordination with the PhD student representative.
  - Please see the [2026 schedule](/phd-seminars/seminar-schedule/).
- You can swap slots when mutually agreed and approved by the PGRC.

### Responsibility of Presenter

- Invite at least one academic staff member.
  - First-year PhD students: invite one of your supervisors.
  - Second-year PhD students and above: invite at least one academic who is NOT your supervisor.
  - You may consider inviting people from other universities.
- You are required to present your own work.
  - If you are in your first year, you may present preliminary results or replication results.
- Apart from your second presentation, which revisits the project of your first, you may not present a project that you have previously presented at the PhD seminar unless you have significant new developments (or it is your job market paper in Year 4).

### Responsibility of Discussant

- Prepare a set of slides that include:
  - a summary of the talk (1 page max);
  - constructive criticisms, questions about the motivation, (interpretation of the) results, etc.

### Responsibility of Other Participants

- Please actively engage in the presentation. Try to be as critical (note: not mean) as possible - bonus points if you can pick apart the substance of the paper (i.e. its motivation, contribution). The aim is to help the presenter improve their research as much as possible.
- If you're attending via Zoom, you are expected to **turn on your camera**.
