You nominate the examiners once your candidate has lodged their Notification of Intention to Submit, which they do at least two months before they plan to submit. The nomination is reviewed by the PGC, the Faculty and the Graduate Research School, and by the Dean of Graduate Research if a conflict of interest is declared. Examiners are only invited once it is approved, and they can decline.

## The sequence

| Step | Who does it |
|------|-------------|
| Notification of Intention to Submit | The candidate lodges it; you review it |
| Nomination of Examiners form | You |
| Review of the nomination | PGC, then Faculty, then the Graduate Research School |
| Any declared conflict of interest | Referred to the Graduate Research School, and may go to the Dean of Graduate Research, who decides |
| Confirming and inviting the examiners | Graduate Research School |
| Panel chair | Nominated by the PGC, confirmed by the Faculty. Usually an academic from the School, independent of the candidate and of the research |

## Who can examine

Each examiner must:

- be an expert in the discipline and academically reputable in the field of the thesis, with a significant body of published work;
- be external to UNSW;
- be independent of the conduct of the research and free from any actual, perceived or potential conflict of interest;
- typically hold a qualification at least equivalent to the degree being examined;
- have previous experience in HDR supervision or examination.

For a doctoral thesis, at least one examiner should be from outside Australia.

"External to UNSW" also excludes anyone holding a current honorary, adjunct, emeritus or conjoint title, and anyone who has been a UNSW employee in the last five years.

## Writing the justification

The Nomination of Examiners form asks you for a justification of each examiner's suitability against those criteria, and a declaration of any potential conflict. "Professor X is a leading scholar in the field" is not a justification. Answer each criterion with something checkable:

- **Expertise, against this thesis.** Name the fields, and connect them to particular chapters. Cite two or three of the examiner's papers that bear on them.
- **Standing.** Position and institution, publication record, editorships, fellowships, prizes.
- **Examination experience.** PhD candidates supervised to completion, theses examined, and where.
- **Independence.** State positively that you have checked, and what you checked.

A model, with the specifics you would fill in:

```
Professor [Name] is [position] at [university, country]. Their work is in
[field], and [paper], [paper] and [paper] bear directly on Chapters 2 and 3,
which develop [topic]. They are [associate editor of [journal] / a fellow of
[society]] and have published in [journals]. They have supervised [n] doctoral
candidates to completion and have examined PhD theses at [institutions].

Neither the candidate nor any member of the supervisory team has co-authored
with them, held a grant with them, or been supervised by or supervised them.
They hold no UNSW appointment. I have found no shared editorial board,
committee or grant panel. There has been no contact with them about this
thesis.
```

Where you do have something to declare, give the facts and leave the assessment alone:

```
[Name] and I have both served on the editorial board of [journal] since
[year]. We have never co-authored, hold no grants together, and board duties
do not involve working together beyond the annual meeting. I am declaring this
so that it can be assessed independently.
```

## Search for the conflicts you have forgotten

Before you nominate, search for connections rather than relying on memory. Put your own name, and each other supervisor's name, together with the potential examiner's name into a search engine or an LLM, and do the same for the candidate's name. Look for:

- co-authored papers, including comments, replies and chapters in edited volumes;
- grants held together, and grant or funding panels you have both sat on;
- editorial boards, committees and society offices held at the same time;
- conferences, workshops or special issues you have organised together;
- doctoral committees and examinations.

The connections that cause trouble are the ones nobody remembers: an editorial board you joined years ago, a chapter in a volume they edited, a panel you both sat on once.

You should also consult your supervisory team and the candidate about potential examiners and whether any conflict exists. If the candidate raises a concern about a potential examiner, document it and avoid nominating that person where you can.

## Conflicts of interest

Three things to hold on to:

- **You do not decide whether a conflict disqualifies someone, and neither does the PGC.** Declare it so that an independent assessment can be made.
- **A conflict does not automatically rule an examiner out.** Many are manageable. What matters is that it is on the record and assessed.
- **Failing to declare one is serious.** If a conflict emerges later, the examiner's report is annulled and a replacement examiner is appointed, months into your candidate's examination.

Relationships that would normally exclude an examiner. "Supervisor" here means anyone in a supervisory capacity, including joint and secondary supervisors and other advisers.

| | Normally excludes an examiner who |
|---|---|
| Co-authorship | has co-authored with the candidate or any supervisor in the last five years |
| Supervision | was a PhD candidate of a supervisor, or co-supervised with one, in the last five years; was on the supervisory team; or has worked with the candidate on the thesis |
| Employment | has employed, or been employed by, the candidate or a supervisor in the last five years; is in negotiation to do so; or has acted as a referee for either |
| Money | holds a current grant with a supervisor; holds a patent with a supervisor granted within the last eight years and still in force; has a business relationship with the candidate or a supervisor in the last five years; or has a direct commercial interest in the research |
| Committees | shares membership of a board or committee with a supervisor, **including editorial boards and grant decision boards** |
| UNSW | is current UNSW staff, holds a current honorary, adjunct, emeritus or conjoint title, was a UNSW employee in the last five years, or has a formal grievance with UNSW |
| Personal | is family or a known relative of the candidate or a supervisor, or is their legal guardian or holds power of attorney |
| The pair | works at the same institution as the other examiner, or has a personal or business relationship with them |

The list is not exhaustive. Anything else that could look like a conflict still has to be declared.

**Bring anything you are unsure about to the PGC before you nominate, not after.** That includes connections you think are too slight to matter: the PGC reviews every nomination and will have to raise it in any case, and it is far cheaper to settle beforehand than to redo a nomination or annul a report.

## After the nomination is approved

Once the examiners are confirmed, the candidate is told their names, and from that point until the examination is over there must be no contact between the examiners and either the candidate or the supervisors. If contact does happen, in either direction, tell the Graduate Research School. In some cases the examination has to be stopped and new examiners appointed.

## The rules

Section 8 of the UNSW [HDR Examination Instruction](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/governance/policy/hub/hdrexamination.pdf) governs nomination. UNSW assesses conflicts using the Australian Council of Graduate Research's [Guidelines for Disclosing and Managing Interests in Graduate Research](https://www.acgr.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/ACGR-Guidelines-for-Disclosing-and-Managing-Interests-in-Graduate-Research-updated.pdf), which set out minor and major conflicts side by side. The Graduate Research School's examiner conflict of interest guide, which the table above follows, is on the [UNSW HDR Hub](https://unsw.sharepoint.com/sites/unswhdr) (UNSW login required).
