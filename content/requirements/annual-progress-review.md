## Timing of the Annual Progress Review (APR)

- After your confirmation review, you have an APR every year. Each APR must be held within **twelve months** of your previous review (either your confirmation review or a previous APR).
- Unlike the confirmation review, you are required to **present** at one of the School's internal seminar series.
- It is possible (even desirable) that you organise your APR before the twelve-month period ends.
  - For example, you may want to give a talk that coincides with the visit of a prominent academic to receive their feedback.

## Procedure for the APR

- Talk to your **supervisors** and the Postgraduate Research Coordinator (PGRC) about the **best timing** for your APR.
  - Do this as soon as is practical, but at least **two months** prior to the scheduled date for your APR.
  - Remember, you have to go through an APR within twelve months of your last review.
  - Pick **tentative dates** that both your supervisors and panel members can attend.
- Once you have tentative dates, contact the School of Economics (SOE) **seminar coordinator** to secure a slot for your presentation.
  - The seminar schedule is usually quite busy, so it is really important that you **plan ahead**.
- If your supervisors find it beneficial to change panel members (e.g., a new staff member or a change in research topic), talk to the academics that you want to have on your panel, check their availability, and more importantly seek feedback on your work.
  - Any change in the panel composition must be approved by the PGRC.
  - Panel members cannot be anyone who has been on your supervisory team, academics supervised by your supervisors, or your supervisors' active research collaborators.
- Once the date is fixed, notify the PGRC.
  - Please notify the PGRC **at least one month in advance** so that the SOE office has enough time to organise things.
  - Specify the date and time of your review.
  - Include the title, abstract and, if available, a draft of the paper you want to present.
- Complete your review form in [GRIS](https://gris.unsw.edu.au/login) (the Graduate Research Information System) at least **one month** before the review, and send relevant documents to your supervisors and panel members. Attach to the form:
  - your progress against the milestones set at your previous review;
  - your presentation slides;
  - evidence of any required UNSW training;
  - the milestones you and your supervisors agree upon for the next 12 months, and a timeline for completing your thesis;
  - the [PhD Program Learning Outcomes (PLO) assessment form](https://unsw-my.sharepoint.com/:w:/g/personal/z3485733_ad_unsw_edu_au/EZdCJAJfIYBCrnbtah0T6gQB1it2PHIJc9ZPkman-gaU5w?e=rxHeNd), signed by your supervisor. This is a **requirement** of the Business School.
- If you miss the review or do not complete the GRIS form, you will receive a show cause request.
- Send the draft of the paper you are presenting to all panel members **at least two weeks** before the review.
  - Ideally, you would include the paper and other relevant information when you fill in the GRIS form.
- Send the presentation material to your supervisors and panel members **at least two days** before the presentation.

## Format of the Review

- You present your own work at one of the School's internal seminar series, for **at least 45–50 minutes**.
  - This is slightly less than the typical academic talk in Australia (60 minutes).
- The panel members will have **10 minutes** to ask questions about your presentation and your research.
  - They will also request an update on the progress you are making towards your degree.
- You will then be asked to leave the room while the panel discusses your progress with your supervisors.
  - You may also meet the panel without your supervisors present.
- Once a decision has been reached, you'll be asked to come back and receive feedback from the panel.
  - The panel will review your **milestones** for the next APR.

## Outcomes of the APR

- The outcome of an APR is **Satisfactory**, **Marginal**, **Unsatisfactory**, or **Unsatisfactory with a request to show cause**.
- After a Marginal or Unsatisfactory outcome, an additional review is held **three months** later (for full-time candidates).

## Minimum Requirements for the APR

- **Coursework**
  - An average of at least 75 in your courses.
- **PhD Seminar Series** (see [PhD Seminars Overview](/phd-seminars/phd-seminars-overview/))
  - Two presentations over the academic year.
  - Discussant duty.
  - At least 80% attendance.
- **Research Progress**
  - You must have accomplished all the **milestones** set up in previous reviews (either your confirmation review or a previous APR).
  - By the end of your **second year**, you must have:
    - the first chapter of your dissertation ready for submission to an academic journal;
    - a draft of the second chapter of your dissertation.
  - By the end of your **third year**, you must have:
    - the second chapter of your dissertation ready for submission to a journal;
    - a draft of your third chapter.
  - You should start thinking about both the job market and your thesis submission early in your fourth year.
- **Presentations Outside the School**
  - By the end of your second year, you must have presented at least once at a conference or at a seminar other than the School's internal seminars.
  - By the end of your third year, you must have presented at least once at an international or domestic conference.
- Requirements of the **Business School** and the Graduate Research School (**GRS**)
  - Completing any required inductions and training.

## Additional Considerations

- If you are tutoring or lecturing (either at UNSW or other institutions), you need to report these activities during your APR.
- Depending on your circumstances, the panel members may advise on the desirability of modifying your teaching load.

For the full rules, see the UNSW [Higher Degree Research Policy](https://www.unsw.edu.au/governance/policy/policy-hub/hdrpolicy) and its [HDR Progression Instruction](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/governance/policy/hub/hdrprogression.pdf). UNSW's policy calls these reviews Research Progress Reviews.
