## MPDBS Year

- Pass all courses with **minimum 85 average** and **minimum 75 marks** for each course.
  - This requirement is **important**. Failure risks not being able to move to the PhD program.
  - You must alert the PGRC as soon as you realise you may be in trouble.
  - Follow this link to look at the [required courses](/requirements/coursework-mpdbs-phd/) for the MPDBS year.
- Do 50 hours of RA work in **Term 2**.
  - You are required to do 25 hours of RA work for two academics in **Term 2.**
  - The purpose of the RA-ship is to give you a chance to collaborate with your potential supervisors and test drive a longer interaction.
  - The PGRC will notify you at the end of Term 1 the names of the two academics you'll be doing RA work.
  - If you have preferences over whom to work with, please discuss it with the PGRC.
  - At the end of T2, you must write a 2-page report on your experience as RA and send it to the PGRC.
    - Deadline for submitting the report is **30 September**.
    - The Business School won't process your payment before you submit your report.
    - Please note you will need to have an TFN (tax file number). Visit the [Australian Tax Office](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals/tax-file-number/apply-for-a-tfn/) for information about applying for a TFN.
- Settle on **supervisors**.
  - After confirming their availability, notify PGRC by the end of **October** the name of your supervisors.
  - Start discussing your **thesis idea** and research topics with your supervisors.
  - Even if by now you only have a vague idea of what you want to do, it is important that you discuss this with your supervisors.
  - Ideally, you have identify a specific research program over the summer before commencing your PhD program.

## Year 1 PhD Program

- Fulfil course requirement with **minimum 75 average**.
  - If you come from the MPDBS program, [follow this link](/requirements/coursework-mpdbs-phd/) for more information about the coursework component.
  - If you are admitted directly to the PhD program, [follow this link instead](/requirements/coursework-direct-entry-phd/).
  - Before enrolling, you must **discuss** your course load with your supervisors and the PGRC.
    - It is possible to defer some courses to your second year.
  - If you are taking courses outside the School of Economics, you need to obtain approval from the LIC (in addition to approval from the PGRC).
  - The UNSW timetable for **2023** can be [found here](https://timetable.unsw.edu.au/2023/subjectSearch.html).
- **Present** and **participate** in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - The PhD seminar series is a **crucial component** of your degree.
  - It gives you a chance to present your research to a friendly audience and receive feedback.
  - It also allows you to give meaningful, well thought feedback to your peers.
  - More information about how the PhD seminars is [available here](/phd-seminars/phd-seminars-overview/).
- Attend as many **School seminars** as possible.
  - Try to meet the speaker and sign up for lunch/dinner, specially if the speaker works in your area of research.
  - In addition to the SOE external seminar (usually Fridays) and internal seminar (usually Wednesdays), keep an eye on seminars in other universities in Sydney (UTS and U of Sydney).
- By the end of **April**, meet with your supervisors to discuss **milestones** for your confirmation review at the end of the year.
- By the end of the year, you should have a concrete **research agenda**, some **preliminary results**, a literature review or a draft of the first chapter of your dissertation.
- In December of your first year, you will be required to go to a [Confirmation Review](/requirements/confirmation-review/) in order to continue your PhD degree.
  - In **late October**, have a chat with the PGRC about the confirmation review process.
  - More information about this process can be [accessed here](/requirements/confirmation-review/).

## Year 2 PhD Program

- Finish taking all your courses.
  - Unless there are exceptional reasons, you should leave Years 3 and 4 exclusively to finish writing your dissertation.
- Present at least at one **seminar** or **domestic conference** (this is part of the [APR requirement](/requirements/annual-progress-review/)).
  - Talk to your supervisors about when and where to present.
  - **Plan ahead**: you should discuss with the PGRC your plans so that sufficient funding can be arranged.
  - Read the [funding rules](/funding/) for details.
- **Present** and **participate** in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - Everything that was stated for Year 1 continues to apply here.
- Attend as many School seminars as possible.
  - Similarly, everything that was stated before in this regard continues to apply here.
- By the middle of Year 2, you should have one full, **ready to submit paper** which can be considered the first chapter of your dissertation.
- By the end of Year 2, you should have a preliminary draft of a **second chapter**, to be discussed during your annual progress review.
- By the end of your second year, you will be required to go through the [Annual Progress Review](/requirements/annual-progress-review/).
  - Talk to your supervisors and the PGRC as early as possible to discuss the **best timing** of your APR.
  - Your APR must be held within twelve months after your confirmation.

## Year 3 PhD Program

- Present at least at one **domestic** or **international conference** (this is part of your [APR requirement](/requirements/annual-progress-review/)).
  - Talk to your supervisors about when and where to present.
  - You are getting closer to your job market. So, presenting at conferences is not just an intellectual pursuit but also a networking opportunity.
  - **Plan ahead**: you should discuss with the PGRC your plans so that sufficient funding can be arranged.
  - Read the [funding rules](/funding/) for details.
- **Present** and **participate** in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - Everything that was stated for Year 1 continues to apply here.
- Attend as many School seminars as possible.
  - Similarly, everything that was stated before continues to apply here.
  - But you should take these opportunities more seriously now, as you are getting closer to your job market.
- By the middle of Year 3, you should have a second, **ready to submit paper**, which can be considered the second chapter of your dissertation.
- By the end of Year 3, you should have a preliminary draft of the third chapter of your dissertation.
  - This work will be the basis of your APR.
  - Crucially, this work should also be the basis of your **job market paper**.
- By the end of your third year, you will be required to go through your second [Annual Progress Review](/requirements/annual-progress-review/).
  - Talk to your supervisors and the PGRC as early as possible to prepare for the APR.
  - This should be your **last APR** before you submit your dissertation.
  - Use it as an opportunity to receive meaningful feedback from your panel.

## Year 4 PhD Program

- Present at least at one **international** or **domestic conference**.
  - Talk to your supervisors about when and where to present.
  - You are getting **really close** to your job market. So, whether you like it or not, attending and presenting at conferences is not just an intellectual pursue but also a networking opportunity. You may meet **potential employers**.
  - **Plan ahead**: you should discuss with the PGRC your plans so that sufficient funding can be arranged.
- Present and participate in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - Everything that was stated for Year 1 continues to apply here.
- Attend as many School seminars as possible.
  - Similarly, everything that was stated before continues to apply here.
  - But you should take these opportunities more seriously now, as soon you will be on the **job market**.
  - Ask the seminar coordinator for a chance to **meet** the speaker and go for lunch/dinner, specially if the speaker is an internationally star working on your field.
- By the **middle of Year 4**, you must have your **job market paper** ready to be submitted.
  - The academic job market for newly minted PhD students in Economics is a well established, well organised institution.
  - Dates are settled and don't change much from year to year.
  - By **October** you should start assembling your job market packages.
  - Discuss with your supervisors and the PGRC about your job prospects.
- Depending on when you started, you will be required to submit your dissertation around September too. So plan ahead!
  - You must follow UNSW [procedure](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/governance/policy/2022-01-policies/thesisexamproc.pdf) to submit your thesis.
