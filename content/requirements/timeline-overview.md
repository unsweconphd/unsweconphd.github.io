The PhD takes 3.5 to 4 years of doctoral research. If you enter through the Master of Pre-Doctoral Business Studies (MPDBS), you first spend one MPDBS year. Years 1 to 4 below count from the start of the PhD itself, not including the MPDBS year.

## MPDBS Year

- Pass all courses with **an average of at least 85** and **at least 75 in each course**.
  - This requirement is **important**. If you don't meet it, you may not be able to move to the PhD program.
  - You must alert the Postgraduate Research Coordinator (PGRC) as soon as you realise you may be in trouble.
  - Follow this link to look at the [required courses](/requirements/coursework-mpdbs-phd/) for the MPDBS year.
- Do 50 hours of research assistant (RA) work in **Term 2**.
  - You are required to do 25 hours of RA work for each of two academics in **Term 2**.
  - The purpose of the RA-ship is to give you a chance to collaborate with your potential supervisors and test drive a longer interaction.
  - At the end of Term 1, the PGRC will tell you the names of the two academics you'll be doing RA work for.
  - If you have preferences over whom to work with, please discuss it with the PGRC.
  - At the end of Term 2, you must write a 2-page report on your experience as an RA and send it to the PGRC.
    - The deadline for submitting the report is **30 September**.
    - The Business School won't process your payment before you submit your report.
    - Please note you will need a tax file number (TFN). Visit the [Australian Taxation Office](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/tax-file-number/apply-for-a-tfn) for information about applying for a TFN.
- Settle on **supervisors**.
  - After confirming their availability, tell the PGRC who your supervisors are by the end of **October**.
  - Start discussing your **thesis idea** and research topics with your supervisors.
  - Even if by now you only have a vague idea of what you want to do, it is important that you discuss this with your supervisors.
  - Ideally, you will have identified a specific research program over the summer before commencing your PhD program.

## Year 1 PhD Program

- Fulfil the course requirement for your entry route.
  - If you come from the MPDBS program, you need **an average of at least 75**. [Follow this link](/requirements/coursework-mpdbs-phd/) for more information about the coursework component.
  - If you are admitted directly to the PhD program, you need **an average of at least 85** in the core courses, **with at least 75 in each**. [Follow this link instead](/requirements/coursework-direct-entry-phd/).
  - Before enrolling, you must **discuss** your course load with your supervisors and the PGRC.
    - It is possible to defer some courses to your second year.
  - If you are taking courses outside the School of Economics (SOE), you need to obtain approval from the course's Lecturer in Charge (LIC), in addition to approval from the PGRC.
  - The current UNSW timetable for Economics courses can be [found here](https://timetable.unsw.edu.au/current/ECONKENS.html).
- **Present** and **participate** in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - The PhD seminar series is a **crucial component** of your degree.
  - It gives you a chance to present your research to a friendly audience and receive feedback.
  - It also allows you to give meaningful, well-considered feedback to your peers.
  - More information about how the PhD seminars work is [available here](/phd-seminars/phd-seminars-overview/).
- Attend as many **School seminars** as possible.
  - Try to meet the speaker and sign up for lunch/dinner, especially if the speaker works in your area of research.
  - In addition to the SOE external seminar (usually Fridays) and internal seminar (usually Wednesdays), keep an eye on seminars in other universities in Sydney (UTS and U of Sydney).
- By the end of **April**, meet with your supervisors to discuss **milestones** for your confirmation review at the end of the year.
- By the end of the year, you should have a concrete **research agenda**, some **preliminary results**, a literature review or a draft of the first chapter of your dissertation.
- In November or December of your first year, you will be required to go to a [Confirmation Review](/requirements/confirmation-review/) in order to continue your PhD degree.
  - In **late October**, have a chat with the PGRC about the confirmation review process.
  - More information about this process can be [accessed here](/requirements/confirmation-review/).

## Year 2 PhD Program

- Finish taking all your courses.
  - Unless there are exceptional reasons, you should leave Years 3 and 4 exclusively to finish writing your dissertation.
- Present at least once at a **conference** or at a **seminar** other than the School's internal seminars (this is part of the [APR requirement](/requirements/annual-progress-review/)).
  - Talk to your supervisors about when and where to present.
  - **Plan ahead**: you should discuss with the PGRC your plans so that sufficient funding can be arranged.
  - Read the [funding rules](/funding/) for details.
- **Present** and **participate** in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - Everything that was stated for Year 1 continues to apply here.
- Attend as many School seminars as possible.
  - Similarly, everything that was stated before in this regard continues to apply here.
- Aim to have one full paper **ready to submit** by the **middle of Year 2**; it can become the first chapter of your dissertation. Your APR at the end of the year requires this chapter to be ready for submission to a journal.
- By the end of Year 2, you must also have a preliminary draft of a **second chapter**, to be discussed during your annual progress review.
- By the end of your second year, you will be required to go through the [Annual Progress Review](/requirements/annual-progress-review/) (APR).
  - Talk to your supervisors and the PGRC as early as possible to discuss the **best timing** of your APR.
  - Your APR must be held within twelve months after your confirmation.

## Year 3 PhD Program

- Present at least once at a **domestic** or **international conference** (this is part of your [APR requirement](/requirements/annual-progress-review/)).
  - Talk to your supervisors about when and where to present.
  - You are getting closer to your job market. So, presenting at conferences is not just an intellectual pursuit but also a networking opportunity.
  - **Plan ahead**: you should discuss with the PGRC your plans so that sufficient funding can be arranged.
  - Read the [funding rules](/funding/) for details.
- **Present** and **participate** in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - Everything that was stated for Year 1 continues to apply here.
- Attend as many School seminars as possible.
  - Similarly, everything that was stated before continues to apply here.
  - But you should take these opportunities more seriously now, as you are getting closer to your job market.
- Aim to have a second paper **ready to submit** by the **middle of Year 3**; it can become the second chapter of your dissertation. Your APR at the end of the year requires this chapter to be ready for submission to a journal.
- By the end of Year 3, you must also have a draft of the third chapter of your dissertation.
  - This work will be the basis of your APR.
  - Crucially, this work should also be the basis of your **job market paper**.
- By the end of your third year, you will be required to go through your second [Annual Progress Review](/requirements/annual-progress-review/).
  - Talk to your supervisors and the PGRC as early as possible to prepare for the APR.
  - If you submit your thesis within twelve months of this review, it will be your **last APR**.
  - Use it as an opportunity to receive meaningful feedback from your panel.

## Year 4 PhD Program

- Present at least once at an **international** or **domestic conference**.
  - Talk to your supervisors about when and where to present.
  - You are getting **really close** to your job market. So, whether you like it or not, attending and presenting at conferences is not just an intellectual pursuit but also a networking opportunity. You may meet **potential employers**.
  - **Plan ahead**: you should discuss with the PGRC your plans so that sufficient funding can be arranged.
- Present and participate in the [PhD seminar series](/phd-seminars/).
  - Everything that was stated for Year 1 continues to apply here.
- Attend as many School seminars as possible.
  - Similarly, everything that was stated before continues to apply here.
  - But you should take these opportunities more seriously now, as you will soon be on the **job market**.
  - Ask the seminar coordinator for a chance to **meet** the speaker and go for lunch/dinner, especially if the speaker is an international star working in your field.
- By the **middle of Year 4**, you must have your **job market paper** ready to be submitted.
  - The academic job market for newly minted PhD students in Economics is a well-established, well-organised institution.
  - Dates are settled and don't change much from year to year.
  - By **October** you should start assembling your job market packages.
  - Discuss your job prospects with your supervisors and the PGRC.
- If you started in Term 1, plan to submit your thesis around **September** of Year 4. UNSW requires a PhD to be completed within 4 years of full-time study.
  - Lodge a Notification of Intention to Submit in GRIS (the Graduate Research Information System) at least **two months** before you submit.
  - If you started your PhD in Term 1 2023 or later, your examination includes an oral component.
  - Follow the UNSW [HDR Examination Instruction](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/governance/policy/hub/hdrexamination.pdf) and the [Thesis Format Guide](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/research/higher-degree-research/thesis-format-guide.pdf) to submit your thesis.
